## Codeforces and LeetCode contest routine

### Participation

- **One suitable Codeforces round weekly:** live when it fits; otherwise an unseen virtual round in Saturday's 06:30–09:00 block. Use the round's actual duration. For a shorter round, use the remaining block for independent attempts; a longer round needs a deliberate block swap.
- **One LeetCode weekly contest:** Sunday 08:00–09:30 IST is reserved for live or virtual practice. Check the official event countdown; this is a planning slot, not a verified upcoming event.
- **LeetCode biweekly is optional:** if its confirmed time is Saturday 20:00–21:30 IST, move the 17:00–18:30 SDE block there and rest during the old block. This keeps Saturday at 9 hours. Use the next coding/review block to upsolve it.
- Live rounds replace an equal amount of scheduled preparation. Do not add them on top of the 5/9-hour target. If two contests clash, select one and take the other virtually.
- Never skip classes or shorten sleep to attend live. Codeforces start times and lengths vary; check the [official contest list](https://codeforces.com/contests), including its timezone conversion links. Check [LeetCode's official contest page](https://leetcode.com/contest/) for registration and the countdown. Exact LeetCode live event times could not be verified from the fetched page on 15 September 2026.

### The practice loop

1. Before a round: choose an appropriate division, prepare your C++ environment, and check the rules.
2. During it: work independently; read carefully, attempt the easiest promising problem, test edge cases, and manage time.
3. After a break: spend the scheduled 90 minutes reviewing. Reattempt the first unsolved problem before consulting an editorial.
4. Close the editorial and implement the idea yourself. Record why you missed it: missing concept, incorrect complexity, implementation bug, or time management.
5. Re-solve at +1, +7, and +21 days in existing coding blocks. A hint-assisted solve is not yet an independent solve.

### Become a stronger coder

- **Weeks 1–2:** take a baseline; get fluent with C++ I/O, STL, references, sorting, and complexity. Use Div. 4/3 material if it matches your current level.
- **Weeks 3–6:** arrays, strings, hashing, prefix sums, binary search, two pointers, and greedy. Add difficulty gradually when you solve current problems independently.
- **Weeks 7–12:** trees, graphs, dynamic programming, and mixed timed practice alongside the main roadmap.
- **Weekly evidence:** contest problems solved independently, wrong submissions, time to first accepted solution, additional problems upsolved, and successful re-solves after seven days.
- Record ratings/ranks for trends, but do not set a promised rating by a deadline. Keep C++/systems, projects, academics, and applications moving too.

### One substantial project

Build a **C++ limit order book and deterministic event replay tool**:

1. Use integer price ticks, stable order IDs, price-time priority, and explicit invalid-input handling.
2. Implement add, cancel, matching, and partial fills.
3. Test duplicate IDs, FIFO ordering, cancellation, crossing orders, and book invariants.
4. Replay synthetic events deterministically and verify final state.
5. Measure throughput and p50/p95/p99 processing latency. Record hardware, compiler/build flags, workload, warm-up, and repeated runs.
6. Compare one optimisation with the same baseline and preserve correctness. Explain what the benchmark cannot establish.

No live trading is required. In SDE interviews, also use your existing full-stack work to explain APIs, databases, deployment, debugging, and ownership. You do not need a second large project just to have another repository.

## A system for days that go wrong

The 43-hour target is a two-week experiment. You cannot guarantee never missing a day. You can make returning easy.

- Define one output before starting: “re-solve binary search,” not “study DSA.” Put the phone out of reach and open only the needed materials.
- Attempt a problem for 25–35 minutes. If stuck, use a hint; then close the solution and reconstruct the idea. Record what you missed.
- Note review dates at +1, +7, and +21 days in the daily journal. Use morning revision time for these rather than adding more sessions.
- **Minimum day:** 15 minutes of familiar revision and 5 minutes choosing the next step. Mark it honestly as Minimum.
- **Rest day:** rest. No artificial check-ins, no punishment session tomorrow.
- **Missed block:** continue with the next scheduled block. Move only genuinely important unfinished work into Sunday’s planning; drop the rest of the backlog.
- **Sunday review:** check academics, sleep, completed prep, mistakes to revisit, application next actions, and whether you had enjoyable time. Pick only three main outcomes for the next week.

At the end of two weeks, check actual focused time, sleep, assignments, and independent re-solves. If you are repeatedly tired, late with academic work, or retaining little, reduce the target instead of extending the day. Consistency should support your life and opportunities, not turn every evening into a test you can fail.
